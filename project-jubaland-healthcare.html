Project and case study

Digitising Public Healthcare in Jubaland

Client: Jubaland Ministry of Health Sector: Healthcare Technology & Digital Transformation Project: Jubaland Digital Health Information System (JDHIS) Status: Pilot planning and implementation

Overview

We are working towards supporting the Jubaland Ministry of Health in advancing the digital transformation of public healthcare through the Jubaland Digital Health Information System (JDHIS).

JDHIS is designed to modernise hospital operations by digitising patient records, clinical workflows, laboratory services, pharmacy management, billing and administrative processes. The platform aims to replace fragmented paper-based workflows with a unified digital system that supports more efficient service delivery, improved information management and stronger institutional oversight.

The initiative is intended to establish a scalable digital foundation that can support public health facilities across Jubaland, beginning with a proposed pilot at Kismayo General Hospital.

The Challenge

Public healthcare facilities that rely heavily on paper-based processes face challenges in maintaining accessible patient records, coordinating clinical services, tracking hospital activity and producing timely operational and financial reports.

As Jubaland advances its hospital cost-recovery policy, reliable digital infrastructure can help facilities implement approved charges consistently, document payments accurately and strengthen financial accountability while maintaining appropriate oversight of exemptions and authorised adjustments.

A connected hospital information system can help bridge the gap between healthcare delivery and financial administration.

Our Solution

We are developing JDHIS as an integrated hospital management and electronic health records platform designed around the operational needs of public healthcare facilities.

  • Digital patient records: Structured patient information to support continuity of care and authorised access to medical histories.
  • Clinical workflow management: Digitised patient registration, outpatient and inpatient workflows, and clinical documentation.
  • Laboratory and pharmacy management: Improved management of laboratory requests, results, medicines and inventory records.
  • Digital billing and cost recovery: Recording services delivered, applying Ministry-approved tariffs, generating bills and documenting authorised exemptions.
  • Mobile-money payment integration: Designed to link hospital bills with payment verification, digital receipts and reconciliation, subject to integration with supported payment providers.
  • Financial reporting and accountability: Reports on billed services, verified collections, outstanding transactions and reconciliation discrepancies.
  • Ministry-level oversight: Aggregated operational and financial information to support planning, monitoring and decision-making.
  • Scalable architecture: A foundation designed to support expansion to additional public facilities and integration with relevant health information systems.

Supporting the Rollout of Hospital Cost Recovery

JDHIS is intended to provide digital infrastructure that can support the Ministry's implementation of its hospital cost-recovery policy.

By linking clinical services with billing and payment records, the platform can help establish a traceable process from the service provided to the charge applied, the payment received and the corresponding financial record.

This can support:

  • Transparent billing: Applying approved tariffs and documenting exemptions and adjustments.
  • Payment traceability: Linking verified mobile-money transactions to the relevant hospital bills and issuing digital receipts.
  • Financial reconciliation: Helping authorised hospital finance personnel compare recorded payments with transaction records and identify discrepancies.
  • Operational visibility: Providing reports that help hospital management and the Ministry understand service activity, billing patterns and collection performance.
  • Evidence-based planning: Producing structured information that can support resource allocation, financial planning and future improvements to hospital services.

The objective is to support a more transparent, accountable and data-informed approach to hospital financing while keeping policy decisions, financial authority and patient-care responsibilities with the appropriate public institutions.

Our Approach

We believe sustainable digital transformation requires more than software. It requires solutions that reflect local operating conditions, fit existing institutional responsibilities and can be supported over time.

Our approach combines locally informed system development, workflow digitisation, staff onboarding, technical support and a phased implementation strategy. The proposed pilot will provide an opportunity to assess operational requirements, gather user feedback and identify improvements before broader deployment.

The platform is being designed with scalability and integration in mind, including the ability to complement existing health reporting systems rather than replace them.

Expected Impact

JDHIS aims to help public healthcare facilities work towards:

  • More accessible and structured patient records.
  • Reduced dependence on paper-based administrative processes.
  • Improved coordination between clinical and administrative departments.
  • More traceable billing and payment records.
  • Stronger financial reporting and accountability.
  • Better visibility into hospital activity and operational needs.
  • A scalable digital foundation for public healthcare transformation across Jubaland.

These are intended outcomes that will be assessed as implementation progresses.

Building the Future of Digital Healthcare

Through JDHIS, we aim to support the Jubaland Ministry of Health in establishing a sustainable digital foundation for public healthcare—connecting patient care, hospital operations and financial accountability within one locally supported platform.

Our vision is to help public health facilities make better use of information, strengthen operational processes and build the digital capabilities needed for long-term healthcare improvement.
